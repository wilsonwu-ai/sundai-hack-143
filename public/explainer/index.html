02

Birthday age vs body age

Birthday age counts candles. Body age tries to say how worn the body is.

A biomarker of aging is a measurement that tries to read that wear. Nobody has one agreed way to do it yet [F46].

  • Speed. The event asks how fast. That is a speedometer. DunedinPACE was built as a rate: it followed 19 organ-system indicators across four time points over two decades [F41].
  • Wear that shows. FaceAge found cancer patients looked 4.79 years older than a non-cancerous reference cohort [F11].
  • Odometer guesses. An open voice model guesses age in years with an error of 7.1 to 10.8 years, depending on dataset. It reads chronological age only [F34].
03

What makes a good one

There is no single agreed checklist yet [F46]. These parts keep showing up in the evidence we found. The Consortium aims at standard, clinically validated ways to measure aging [F53].

  • Repeatable

    Test it again and get about the same result. DunedinPACE showed high test-retest reliability [F42].

  • Linked to what happens next

    It goes with illness, disability or death later on [F42]. DNAm PhenoAge predicts all-cause mortality, cancers, healthspan, physical functioning and Alzheimer's disease [F43].

  • Adds more than the birthday

    The link holds after age is accounted for. Example: a failed 10-second one-leg stand stayed linked to mortality after adjusting for age, sex, BMI and comorbidities, in adults aged 51 to 75 [F21].

  • Shows its sources and limits

    A result that names its study, its cohort and its error can be checked. One that does not cannot, and the field already lacks shared standards [F46].

04

The menu

  • FaceAge

    Reads: a face photo.

    Cancer patients looked 4.79 years older than a non-cancerous reference cohort [F11]. Older FaceAge went with worse survival in 6,196 cancer patients [F13].

    The code is public, for research only [F14]. Weights link not confirmed.

    Demo in 60 s: yes

  • HandAge

    Reads: a photo of the back of the hand.

    No hand-age evidence is established in our research, and no model weights were found. Hand photos can identify a person, as faces can [F16].

    Demo in 60 s: no

  • Wearables

    Reads: watch or ring data collected over days.

    Not established. Our files hold no study showing that a wearable composite tracks aging.

    Demo in 60 s: noneeds a personal data export

  • Voice

    Reads: a short voice clip.

    An open model guesses age in years with an error of 7.1 to 10.8 years, depending on dataset [F34].

    Chronological age only, so a weak aging proxy [F34].

    Demo in 60 s: yes

  • Movement

    Reads: camera video of a simple test, such as a one-leg stand.

    Not completing a 10-second one-leg stand went with higher all-cause mortality, HR 1.84 (95% CI 1.23 to 2.78), in 1702 adults aged 51 to 75 [F21].

    Our pick builds on this one.

    Demo in 60 s: yes

  • Sleep

    Reads: sleep timing from a tracker.

    Sleep regularity predicted mortality better than sleep duration [F32] unverified.

    The cohort averaged 62.8 years and used 7 days of wrist data. Do not apply it to adults aged 20 to 70 [F32].

    Demo in 60 s: noneeds a health export

  • Heart signals

    Reads: pulse from a fingertip on the phone camera, or from face video.

    Fingertip pulse matched resting ECG heart rate (r=.997, RMSE 1.03 beats/min) in healthy young adults. Face pulse was less accurate [F35].

    That checks heart rate accuracy. Our files hold no evidence that camera pulse tracks aging.

    Demo in 60 s: yesfingertip

  • Fitness

    Reads: strength and stamina tests, such as chair stands.

    In 2207 adults aged 60 and over, a fitness age running ahead of birthday age went with depressive symptoms (aOR 1.88) and cognitive impairment (aOR 1.76) [F26].

    A snapshot study. Korean fitness-age equations came from 501,774 people [F25].

    Demo in 60 s: yeschair stands only

  • Combinations

    Reads: two or more signals at once.

    A blood-protein study found nearly 20% of people show strongly accelerated age in one organ and 1.7% in several. That favors a profile of parts over one number [F45].

    No published method in our files merges face, movement and pulse into one age.

    Demo in 60 s: nothree captures will not fit

What wins tonight

The room votes after the 20:00 presentations. That comes from Wilson's notes. Sundai's own judging rules are not confirmed. [F51]

  • Voters try it on themselves

    A demo each person runs on their own phone in under a minute gets remembered. A slide does not. [F51]

  • Honest science

    Researchers and clinicians are in the room, and the Consortium conference starts the next day [F52]. Show sources, error and limits [F42] [F46].

  • Linked to outcomes

    A guess of age in years looks thin: the open voice model errs by 7.1 to 10.8 years [F34]. A link to mortality or illness looks more like a biomarker [F42] [F43].

  • Live on a public URL by 20:00

    The format is a new AI app built from scratch by the end of the day. Broken or offline on stage is out. [F51]

  • Looks new

    FaceAge code is public [F14], so expect several selfie-age teams. Phone apps for chair rise [F55] and fingertip body age [F56] already exist. Credit them openly.

06

Our pick

Movement Age

Prop up your phone, do a one-leg stand and five chair stands. On-device pose estimation places each result on a published age-band reference table, with the source shown and nothing leaving the device.

An estimate from published reference data. Not a medical device, not a diagnosis, and no replacement for a clinician.

How it works

Read in order: left to right, then down.

  1. 1

    Open the page

    Public GitHub Pages URL. A start screen says nothing leaves the device and that this is not a medical device. You pick sex and age band, because the studies report by both [F22] [F23].

  2. 2

    Frame the body

    Prop the camera. A live skeleton appears. The app waits until hips, knees and ankles are visible.

  3. 3

    One-leg stand

    Timer runs while one foot is up. Best of three, like the reference protocol [F22]. The 10-second mark lights up, the threshold studied in adults 51 to 75 [F21].

  4. 4

    Chair stands

    Reps are counted from hip and knee angles. The app reports the 5-rep time or the 30-second count [F23]. A friend can tap each rep to compare counts.

  5. 5

    Look up the band

    Each result is compared with age-band tables from the full texts of [F22] and [F23]. It names the band whose typical result matches. It never gives one year.

  6. 6

    Result card

    Balance and leg strength as two separate bars, each naming its study, cohort and limits. Ends with: an estimate from published reference data, not a medical device, not a diagnosis.

Biggest open item. Neither abstract gives per-band numbers, so the tables must be read from the full texts and typed in, by about 13:00. If that fails, the fallback shows raw time and reps with the study bands described, and no placement [F22] [F23].

Why this one

  1. Every voter can test themselves in the room, and both tests have published reference values across adult ages [F22] [F23].
  2. The one-leg stand has a mortality link after adjusting for age, sex, BMI and comorbidities. It covers ages 51 to 75 only, so it appears as context, never as a prediction about the user [F21].
  3. Remote video sit-to-stand showed good to moderate validity against in-person testing [F24]. A live tap check turns that into a visible measured error, which fits the reliability-first template this crowd uses [F42] [F46].
  4. It fits one day: a static site, pose estimation on the device, no server, no training [F51].
  5. It stays away from the crowd most likely to build selfie age and from FaceAge's research-only terms [F14].

Scores for Movement Age

Kill criteria

Key: pass pending fail Any fail means re-score before pitching.

  • K1pass

    A published reference table for the chosen tests covers adults from roughly 20 to 80.

    Passes on coverage. One-leg norms: 549 healthy adults aged 18 and over, six bands from 18 to 39 up to 80 and over [F22]. Chair-stand percentiles by sex and age: 393 Colombian adults, though the age range could not be confirmed in the fetched text [F23]. The per-band numbers are not in hand. The youngest band spans 18 to 39, so results are bands, not years.

  • K2passnarrow pass

    Evidence that the measure predicts mortality or illness beyond birthday age.

    One-leg stand: HR 1.84 (95% CI 1.23 to 2.78) in 1702 adults aged 51 to 75, adjusted for age, sex, BMI and comorbidities [F21]. Do not extend it below 51. Chair stands: the only outcome link is a fitness-age composite in adults 60 and over [F26]. Nothing in our files covers ages 20 to 50.

  • K3pending

    The 10:30 intro does not hand out data, a model or a prize that favors another modality.

    Judge it live. No co-host or speaker is listed [F51]. If the intro releases a face or wearable dataset, or a prize for one modality, re-score before the 11:00 pitch.

  • K4pending

    No teammate brings a better idea at 11:00.

    Judge it at team formation. Score any rival idea on the same five criteria, with live demo and science weighted most.

How it differs from what exists

A camera sit-to-stand app already exists. The iOS Sit To Stand App scores the chair rise from 240 frames-per-second video and reports time, velocity and muscle power [F55]. Its "scientifically validated" label is the developer's own claim, and it runs on Apple devices only. Movement Age names it as prior art, in the README and on stage, and does not compete on biomechanics.

What differs: Movement Age runs in any phone or laptop browser with no install. It pairs a balance test with the chair test. It places each result on published age-band tables and shows the source study and cohort. The F55 finding does not describe an age-referenced readout.

It also differs from AgeQ, a phone body-age app that uses fingertip pulse and an activity questionnaire [F56]. Movement Age shows its sources and its measured counting error, and gives no single body-age score.

The 20:00 demo

  1. 1
    0 to 10 sA QR code on screen. "Open this on your phone now. No install, nothing leaves your device."
  2. 2
    10 to 25 sA volunteer does a one-leg stand on stage. The live skeleton shows, the timer runs, the 10-second mark lights up, and the result appears as an age band with its source study [F22].
  3. 3
    25 to 40 sFive chair stands. The camera count and a teammate's tap count appear side by side with the agreement figure, so the room sees the measured error [F24].
  4. 4
    40 to 50 sThe room tries it. Voters do a one-leg stand at their seats, holding a chair back, and see their own result card.
  5. 5
    50 to 60 sA credibility card lists what each number rests on: [F22], [F23], and [F21], limited to ages 51 to 75. It credits [F55] as prior art and shows the line: an estimate from published reference data, not a medical device. Then the open-source repo link and the ask for votes.

Safety: a one-leg stand can cause a fall. Tell everyone to stand next to a wall or a chair back.

09

Still unknown

  • Can the full texts of the 2007 one-leg stand norms [F22] and the Colombian sit-to-stand values [F23] be opened today? Are their age bands fine enough to be useful?
  • What does the app show users under 40, since the youngest one-leg band spans 18 to 39 [F22]?
  • Does in-browser pose estimation count reps and detect foot-down on ordinary phone cameras in the venue's lighting, and from what distance?
  • What chair height and arm position do the tests in [F23] use? That changes how comparable a voter's result is.
  • Will the 10:30 intro release data, a model or a prize that favors another modality (K3)?
  • What are Sundai's real judging and voting rules, team-size rule and open-source requirement? Only Wilson's notes say.
  • How many other teams pick movement or sit-to-stand? That decides how much the tap check and the sources card must carry.

Gaps in the research: no study was found that validates pose estimation for rep counting or balance timing. No evidence was obtained for the one-leg stand below age 51. No current permanent FaceAge weights link was confirmed. No hand-age model or code was found.
